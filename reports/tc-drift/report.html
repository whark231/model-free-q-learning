Final agent runs: 4 cost levels × 10 seeds

In plain terms: the agent loses to the oracle at every cost level, mostly because it heads for a slightly wrong portfolio. At zero cost, $100M and $1B it is 10 to 13 bps/yr behind the oracle and also loses to always rebalancing. At $10B, where trading costs matter most, it learns when to trade: +5.0 bps/yr against always rebalancing and 2.8 behind the oracle. Of the 12 expectations registered before the runs, 8 are met, 4 partly met and 0 not met.

Where the gap to the oracle comes from

The agent's gap to the oracle splits exactly into three paired terms (design §7, TC-3), checked on every path: where the agent heads (rebalancing to its own favourite target against rebalancing to the best target), when it trades (the agent against rebalancing to its own target), and the oracle's own lead over always rebalancing. At the three lower levels almost the whole gap is where it heads. At $10B the timing term is large and positive.

Settings and provenance

10 seeds per level, 4,000,000 training blocks per run (restart every 90 blocks), master seed 20261016. Hyperparameters (tuned on seed 20261112 at $1B, design §9): α_value = 0.0003, α_cost = 0.0003, β = 2.303e-06 (ε reaches 1% at halfway), RLS on the cost block after 301,030 updates with prior 0.001·I, target refresh every 1,000 blocks, l2 = 0.

Commit 1787f0dd56, clean tree, Python 3.13.9, numpy 2.3.5, arm64, BLAS pinned to one thread; about 20 minutes a run with seven in parallel. Per-path values were recomputed independently from values.npz and match the summaries within 1e-13 (results/tc-drift/runs/README.md).

Post hoc relative to baseline-v1. Benchmark information (the true costs, the target error from μ and Σ) informed the learning-rule choice, the tail averaging and the D12 restatements (design §14 risk 5); the agent itself never sees μ, Σ, κ, λ or the oracle.
