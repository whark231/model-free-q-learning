**Key concepts to understand**

- **Tick and tie.** Take a number printed in a document, find the file it came from, and recompute it. If the two agree the number "ties". Every number I tied is listed in §7.
- **Pre-registered vs post hoc.** A setting fixed before seeing results cannot have been picked to flatter them. A setting chosen afterwards can, so it must be labelled. The four `baseline-v1` experiments are pre-registered (git confirms the order). Everything else is post hoc and the report must say so each time.
- **Benchmark information in a design choice.** The agent never sees μ, Σ or the optimum. People did, when sizing the training budget and designing the transaction-cost extension. That is allowed, but the assignment's leakage rule means each case needs a sentence of disclosure.
- **Justified vs recorded.** `src/config.py` records that an episode is 20 days. A justification says why 20 and not 5 or 63. The assignment grades the second.

**Key takeaways**

- No blocker in code or results. The blockers are the calendar: the report, and the TC runs (go/no-go 2026-11-24).
- The largest rubric exposure is Part III's "Report and justify" list and the absence of any methods write-up for Part II.
- The budget-only closed-form portfolio is verified in tests but appears in no committed table. The report needs one.
- Three FA explanations rest on calculations with no committed script. Report them as hypotheses or commit the scripts.
